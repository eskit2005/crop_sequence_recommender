## 2. Agro-Ecological Profiling of the Northern Granary

The Northern Granary and Rangpur/Dinajpur Piedmont region represents Bangladesh's pre-eminent agro-climatic corridor for non-paddy field crops, tubers, and temperate vegetables. The audited batch spans three distinct Agro-Ecological Zones (AEZs):

### 2.1 AEZ 1: Old Himalayan Piedmont Plain
- **Districts Covered**: Dinajpur (11 locations), Panchagarh (2 locations), Thakurgaon (2 locations).
- **Geomorphology & Elevation**: Gently undulating alluvial piedmont apron sloping southward from the Himalayan foothills (elevation 30 m to 85 m above sea level).
- **Soil Pedology & Infiltration**: Non-Calcic Brown Floodplain Soils and Black Terai Soils. Texture ranges from coarse loamy sand to friable silt loam. Soils possess exceptionally high saturated hydraulic conductivity ($K_{sat} > 25\text{ cm/day}$), moderate-to-low cation exchange capacity (CEC), and rapid internal drainage.
- **Hydrological Land-Type Compliance**: Classified predominantly as **High Land (HL)** (unflooded throughout the monsoon) and **Medium High Land (MHL)** (intermittent shallow ponding $< 30\text{ cm}$).
  > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **The High Land Boro Prohibition Rule**: In High Land parcels of Dinajpur, Thakurgaon, and Panchagarh, unconfined aquifer irrigation of puddled wetland rice (*Boro dhan*) during Rabi is catastrophic. Excessive downward percolation causes water loss rates exceeding 15–25 mm/day, precipitating rapid groundwater water-table drawdown and pump cavitation. The BARC CZIS rules and our Stage 3 engine strictly prohibit *Boro dhan* on HL, requiring deep-rooting or non-flooded C3/C4 staples (*Potato, Sweet Potato, Wheat, Maize, Mustard, Cole Vegetables*). All 15 HL locations audited fully complied with this hard biophysical rule.

### 2.2 AEZ 3: Tista Meander Floodplain
- **Districts Covered**: Rangpur (Taraganj, Badarganj).
- **Geomorphology & Pedology**: Level to gently undulating floodplain landscape composed of young, highly fertile alluvial silts and olive-brown silt loams.
- **Land-Type Hydrology**: Medium High Land (MHL) with moderate internal drainage. Soils can sustain bunded standing water during July–October monsoon rainfall, making MHL ideally suited for *T. Aman dhan* in Kharif-2, preceded by intensive cash crops (*Potato, Winter Maize, Vegetables*) in Rabi and *Jute, Sesame, T. Aus* in Kharif-1.

### 2.3 AEZ 25 & AEZ 26: Level & High Barind Tract Edge
- **Districts Covered**: Bogura (Adamdighi, Nandigram), Joypurhat (Panchbibi).
- **Geomorphology & Pedology**: Pleistocene terrace landscape featuring grey terrace soils and weathered clay-loam topsoils underlain by a dense, impermeable ploughpan (at 10–15 cm depth).
- **Hydrological Dynamics**: While High Land parcels exhibit moisture stress in late winter, Medium High Land (MHL) parcels retain surface water during monsoon rains, providing an ideal substrate for *T. Aman dhan* in Kharif-2 and early cash potato/mustard in Rabi.

## 5. Cross-Cutting Regional Insights & Algorithmic Findings

### 5.1 Biophysical Rule Verification: 100% Elimination of Boro on High Land
The foremost biophysical objective of this audit was to inspect whether the Stage 3 candidate pool engine successfully enforces hydrological land-type constraints. In the Northern Granary, 15 out of 20 benchmark locations are classified as **High Land (HL)** in the BARC CZIS database. Historically, excessive groundwater pumping for dry-season Boro rice on porous HL sandy loams has lowered water tables by up to 0.5–1.0 m annually in Dinajpur and Thakurgaon.

The audit confirms **100% compliance** across all 15 HL locations:
- Zero instances of *Boro dhan* appeared in Rabi.
- In their place, the engine prioritized agronomically and economically superior upland alternatives: *Potato, Sweet Potato, Wheat, Winter Maize, Mustard, Cole Vegetables (Broccoli, Cabbage, Cauliflower), Linseed, and Sunflower*.

### 5.2 Dynamic Area Ranking vs Regional Staple Dynamics (The Panchbibi Case Study)
In Location 20 (**Panchbibi Upazila, Joypurhat District**, Medium High Land), the candidate selector generated the following Kharif-2 pool:
1. *Cotton* (Malvaceae, 150d, Tier: Ideal, Effective Area: 13,236 ha)
2. *Chilli* (Solanaceae, 110d, Tier: Ideal, Effective Area: 10,496 ha)
3. *Black Gram* (Fabaceae, 75d, Tier: Ideal, Effective Area: 9,938 ha)

While *T. Aman dhan* is physically suitable in Panchbibi on MHL, its recorded effective area in the BARC CZIS database was 5,036 ha. Because the Stage 3 bucketer orders candidates by `effective_area_ha` descending, the three top-ranked cash crops crowded out *T. Aman* from the 3-crop Kharif-2 bucket. While these three upland cash crops are highly viable and profitable on well-drained MHL soils, *T. Aman* remains the staple food crop in Joypurhat. Consequently, Panchbibi was classified as **ACCEPTABLE** rather than VALIDATED to document this algorithmic sensitivity.
