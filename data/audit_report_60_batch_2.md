## 1. Executive Summary

This independent scientific audit evaluates the crop recommendations and rotational parameters generated by the **Crop Sequence Recommender Engine** across 15 representative locations in Northwestern and Southwestern Bangladesh (Batch 2: `loc_16` through `loc_30`). The audited locations span three distinct and critically important agro-ecological domains:

1. **The High Barind Tract (AEZ-26)**: Paba (`loc_16`), Durgapur (`loc_17`), Gomastapur (`loc_18`), Shibganj (`loc_19`), Porsha (`loc_20`), and Niamatpur (`loc_21`). This region is characterized by undulating, highly weathered grey terrace clays and red clay loams, acidic topsoil with low organic matter (<1.2%), low annual rainfall (~1,200–1,400 mm), and severe winter moisture stress mitigated primarily by deep tubewells (DTWs) operated by the Barind Multipurpose Development Authority (BMDA).
2. **The Little Jamuna Floodplain & Chalan Beel Basin (AEZ-4, AEZ-5, AEZ-11)**: Badalgachhi (`loc_22`), Baraigram (`loc_23`), Chatmohar (`loc_24`), and Sujanagar/Bera (`loc_25`). This region exhibits extreme hydrological variation, ranging from well-drained alluvial silt loams to deeply flooded wetland depressions (*beels*) governed strictly by flood recession schedules.
3. **The High Ganges River Floodplain & Western Border Cash Belt (AEZ-11)**: Kumarkhali (`loc_26`), Alamdanga (`loc_27`), Jibannagar (`loc_28`), Kotchandpur (`loc_29`), and Bagherpara (`loc_30`). This zone features highly fertile, calcareous brown and dark grey floodplain soils, high cropping intensities (>210–250%), and commercial cash crop rotations including sugarcane, maize, sunflower, pulses, vegetables, and fiber crops.

Every candidate recommendation was cross-verified against official reports from the **Department of Agricultural Extension (DAE)**, research bulletins of the **Bangladesh Agricultural Research Institute (BARI)** (specifically the On-Farm Research Division, OFRD), **Bangladesh Rice Research Institute (BRRI)** cropping systems publications, **Bangladesh Agricultural Research Council (BARC)** agro-ecological matrices, and international agricultural databases (FAO, ICRISAT, SciSpace).
